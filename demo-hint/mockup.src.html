What ended it, and what the tap did

Every rule in §3 is invisible when it works, which is why they are the ones that get quietly broken. So the phone says it out loud.

Waiting for the hint. Then tap something.

    What to look for

    Three things this drawing exists to settle, and one it cannot.

    Look atBecause
    Study A on its own The ring is the more minimal answer and it may simply be too quiet to mean anything to somebody who has never seen this app. Watch it once without knowing where to look. If your eye does not go to the corner, A is not the answer, however much nicer it is.
    The caption at 375pt Narrow the window until the phone is the width of an SE. "Your account lives here." is the one that will wrap first, and a hint that wraps is a hint that reads as a paragraph.
    Tapping a card through the caption The card presses, the hint leaves, and the log says both. That is pointer-events: none doing the only job it has. This is the line that should never be traded for a dismiss button.
    The rectangle, shown Turn on Show the rectangle and tap inside it. On Home the caption lands in the gap between the bar and the greeting, so what goes through is bare paper, which is a good sign about the placement and a poor demonstration. The rule earns its keep one control up: the ring is drawn on the avatar button, and tapping the disc mid ring still opens Profile. That is the tap that would be swallowed if these pseudo elements ever took a pointer.
    Dark, in a dark room --hc-gold deepens to #CBAC74 on charcoal so it glows rather than glares. The ring is the only gold thing on the screen either way, which is what makes it findable.
    Reduce Motion No ring, no travel, the caption simply present and held longer. The rail refuses outright under Reduce Motion because it is decoration. This is information, so it degrades instead. Study A has nothing left to show here, which is an argument against shipping A alone.
    What the drawing cannot settle. Whether 2500ms is the right wait, and whether six seconds is the right hold. Both are opening guesses and both feel different in a hand, at arm's length, in the sun, held by somebody who has not seen the app before. Take it outside before deciding.
    What is faked here. The splash is abbreviated, Home is a sketch, and the three launch counter of §3d is not modelled at all: this phone hints every time you ask it to, because a study you have to reinstall to see twice is a study nobody looks at twice. Everything else, the tokens, the 42px disc, the 20px gutter, the 52px bar, the durations and the death list, is the shipping thing.
