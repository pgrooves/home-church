The interaction, exactly

What the phone above is doing, in the order it does it.

  • Trigger Tapping ••• lifts the sheet out of the bar over 400ms. The tile stays on ••• the whole time it is open.
  • Dismiss · 1 Drag the handle down. The panel tracks the finger and the scrim fades with it; past a third of the panel, or on a flick, it goes. Let go early and it springs back.
  • Dismiss · 2 Tap the paper behind it. Esc does the same on a keyboard.
  • Dismiss · 3 Tap another tab. The scrim stops short of the bar on purpose, so the bar never goes dead under an open sheet.
  • Choose Tapping a module closes the sheet and lands on it. The ••• tile stays raised, because you are somewhere, not lost.
  • Swipe Sideways drag runs the whole row, tabs and modules alike. The sheet is a menu, not a stop on the way.

Where the swipe goes now

Five tabs become seven stops. The tile only has six places to be, so it parks on ••• and stays there across every module.

Left off Connect brings Journal in the way any other tab arrives. Right off Journal goes back to Connect. The far ends still resist and stop.

What building it touches

Five files, and the geometry of the bar is not one of them.

FileChange

Four calls to make before it is built

  • Which grid. Filling the width is the default here, since two narrow tiles floating in a wide panel is the empty space you asked not to have. Four across is the alternative: tiles never change size as modules are added.

    Filling the width means today's two tiles are wider than tomorrow's four.

  • Flush or raised. Flush is the default here because the bar already has a rule about what a tile means, and it means you are here. Raised spends that tile on all of them at once, which makes the targets louder and the sheet heavier.

    Raised looks better at eight modules than at two.

  • Whether the More screen survives. It can stay reachable at ?v=more for old links while ••• stops opening it, or go entirely and take the leader-mode row with it.

    Leader mode is the only thing on it that is not in the grid.

  • What the sheet does on a keyboard and a screen reader. Drawn here as a non-modal panel: focus moves in, Esc closes, the bar stays reachable. A true modal would trap focus and take the bar with it.
