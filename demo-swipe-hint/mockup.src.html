The swipe hint

The five tabs swipe, and the row carries on past Connect into the modules behind the ••• tile. Nothing on any screen says so. This draws the hint for it: the screen leans toward the next tab and comes back, twice, on the same clock the index rail already hints on.

The phone really swipes. Drag it sideways and the gesture underneath carries the same numbers js/swipe.js does, so the hint can be watched against the movement it is teaching. Using it retires the hint, which is the rule, and which is also the fastest way to stop the page doing anything at all — press Replay.

The next screen really drawn is what ships. Paper slides over paper, so a lean over nothing reads as the app hiccupping however deep it goes, and the seam alone only fixes that in the light: the shadow .hc-swipe__pane draws is tuned against cream and very nearly vanishes in Dark. Real content is its own edge in both, and it says the thing the other two only gesture at. It costs one screen render per hint, which is what a single swipe already pays.

64px is the first number that clears the 20px page gutter with enough left over, about 44px, to show the first few characters of the next screen's heading. Under that you are looking at blank margin. Committing a swipe takes 26% of the width, about 102px here, so 64 is still clearly an offer rather than the app changing tabs and thinking better of it.

Once can read as a glitch. Evenly twice reads as a machine ticking. The second one smaller reads as a thumb testing and settling, which is the thing being described.

Out is quicker than back in both. Leaving is deliberate, returning is a release. Neither overshoots: the design system §3g rules out springs, and out-and-back already feels elastic without one.

It leans toward whatever is actually there. On Home that is left. On the last stop in the row there is nothing further left, so it leans right instead. Inside a guide nothing swipes at all, so it refuses and says so.

Both at once is on the page so the argument against it is legible rather than asserted. Two things moving is a tutorial, and the page sliding under the notches drags the swell with it.

This hint refuses outright under Reduce Motion rather than degrading, which is the rail's rule and not the guide hint's. The guide hint degrades because it is information and there is something left of it when it holds still. This one is entirely movement. There is nothing left.

What it did, and why

Every rule is invisible when it works. This says which one spoke.

    Worth doing in this order

    1. Watch it once with the seam off, then once with the paper edge on. That is the whole question this page exists for.

    2. Switch the phone to Dark and do it again. The shipping seam is a shadow tuned against cream paper.

    3. Actually swipe the phone. The hint stops for good, because you have found the thing it was pointing at. Replay puts it back; on a real phone only relaunching does.

    4. Put a finger down in the middle of a lean and drag. The gesture takes the offset over from where the hint had it rather than snapping to zero first.

    5. Set it to Inside a guide. Nothing happens, and the log says why by name.

    What is faked

    Five screens, sketched. Enough that sliding shows you a different one. The row in the app runs past these into the modules behind ••• and then Settings.

    The gesture reads pointers, not touches. The real one reads touches, because the index rail and the pull to refresh are reading the same finger and a pointer stream does not survive a moving page. That is the largest thing in here that is not the shipping build.

    The rail's notches are drawn, not live. Its swell is the real gaussian at the real numbers; what it does not do is read the headings off the page.

    Nothing is retired across launches, because a study cannot show you a thing not happening.

    What this cannot settle

    Whether it is worth building at all. HINTS.md §12 says the sideways drag is discovered by accident more than anything else in the app, and that is an argument for leaving it alone. This page can tell you whether the drawing is good. It cannot tell you whether the hint is needed.

    Whether it stutters. Nothing here animates a background and nothing is drawn over the plinth, which is the shape §12 asks for. That is a shape argument, not a measurement, and §12 is explicit that the measurement is the part that was owed. Open this on a real phone, in the sun, at arm's length.

    How it feels on the twentieth time. Thirty seconds apart, for as long as somebody has not swiped, is a lot of leaning. A week in a pocket answers that and an afternoon at a desk does not.
