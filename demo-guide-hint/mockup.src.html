The Seat at the Table

What a forgotten grandson of a dead king teaches us about grace

David finally has everything, and in the middle of it he asks a question nobody asked him to ask. Is there anyone left of Saul’s house I can show kindness to?

Second Samuel 9 opens on a rare moment of calm. David has come through the cave years, the civil war, and the consolidation of the kingdom. He is secure. And security usually makes people protective, not generous.

His question is the hinge of the whole story. Is there still anyone left of the house of Saul, that I may show him kindness for Jonathan’s sake? The word behind kindness is hesed, the covenant loyalty of God, the stubborn love that keeps showing up after the relationship has given it every reason to quit.

Think about the last time somebody was kind to you and you had not done a single thing to earn it. What did you do with it?

What happened

    Why the sentence highlights itself

    Highlighting is the least discoverable thing in the app. The bar that offers Note this is docked above the tab bar and only exists once something is already selected, so there is nothing on screen to find. Nothing can be pointed at, so the hint performs it instead.

    The marker is drawn line by line from the left, the way a thumb would drag it, and it is painted under the prose so the words sit on top of it rather than behind a film of it.

    This changes the app's real highlight

    What the app draws today is the second option in The highlight: a wash in the bottom of the line, closer to an underline than a marker. Switch between them and watch the existing highlight in the Overview change with it.

    Making the hint a block behind the words means changing .hc-hl in css/components.css, which restyles every highlight anybody has already made. Worth doing, probably, but it is a change to the app and not to the hint.

    It stays, and it never takes a tap

    Every launch, on the first folded section opened, it shows and then waits. No timer. Scrolling does not put it away either, because it is drawn in the page's own coordinates and travels with the sentence. A tap ends it, and switching Hints off ends it.

    pointer-events: none on both layers and every child, so the tap that ends it also lands on whatever was under it. Tap the words through the card and the log says so. There is no Got it and no x: going on with what you were doing is how this app puts things away.

    What the sweeps trade away

    E, F and G have no words, so there is nothing to wait for a tap: they play once and are gone. That is the appeal and it is also the cost. A person looking at the heading they just tapped can miss the whole thing, and a marker that ran while they were looking elsewhere leaves no trace that it ever ran.

    It is also the one shape Reduce Motion cannot carry. There is no still version of a movement. Switch Reduce Motion on with a sweep selected and watch what is left: the marker simply appears and goes, which is a different hint wearing the same name.

    Reduce Motion degrades, never refuses

    This hint is information, not decoration, so stillness must not cost it. Under Reduce Motion the marker is simply present and the words simply present, with nothing drawing itself. Same words, same place, no movement. Switch it in the rail and replay.

    The switch, and why it says so

    One switch in Your account → Display for every hint there will ever be. Turn it off in the phone and open a section: the log says Hints is off rather than saying nothing.

    A hint that fails to appear looks exactly like a hint that is switched off, which is what cost the last attempt at this a revert. The app should always be able to say which.

    What is faked here

    The guide is three sections rather than six, the tab bar does not navigate, and the sentence the hint picks is fixed. Everything else is the real thing: the tokens, the 20px gutter, the 52px bar, the fold animation, the wash, and the docked bar's true position above the plinth.

    What this cannot settle

    Whether the beat after the fold is right, and whether six seconds is. Both feel different in a hand, at arm's length, in the sun, held by somebody who has not seen the app. The last attempt at hints was green in a headless browser and wrong on a phone. Open this on the phone before picking.
