How a Thursday night goes

  • Open

    Trey has leader mode on, so the Group tab offers Host tonight. Tapping it mints a six digit code and opens a room against this week's guide. Nobody else has to do anything yet.

  • Text

    Text the code to your group hands a prefilled message to the phone's share sheet. That plumbing already exists as HC.native.shareText, used today by the quote cards, so this is one call and no new dependency.

  • Join

    Everyone else opens the Group tab, types the six digits, and is in. The tab remembers the room, so nobody types it twice, and it clears itself when the room closes.

  • Carried over

    The room opens holding this week's discussion questions, copied out of the guide at the moment the room is created. Same questions, same order, same headings, no retyping.

  • Edited

    Trey can reword a question, drop one, or add his own. Every change lands on every phone in the room. The published guide is untouched, because one leader's night should not rewrite the guide for the other three groups.

  • Answered

    Under each question, everyone gets a box. Post and it appears for the whole room under your first name, taken straight from your profile. You can edit and delete your own. Nobody can edit anyone else's, and the host can take anything down.

Four things to decide

Writing needs an account, reading does not

A note has to carry a first name and an owner, and the app cannot tell who you are unless you are signed in. The room stays readable to anyone with the code, and the first attempt to post is what asks you to sign in.

It catches up, it is not instant

The app talks to Supabase with plain fetch and carries no SDK. Polling every few seconds while the tab is open, plus a pull on focus, keeps that true. Realtime over a websocket is the other answer and it is a bigger change than it sounds.

Host is a row, not a switch

Leader mode today is a checkbox in Profile that anybody can flip. The room's host has to be a host_id the database enforces, or every guest can rewrite the questions.

Codes expire at midnight

Six digits is a million rooms, which is plenty when only tonight's are live. Enforce uniqueness among open rooms only, and let last week's code be somebody else's this week.

The one that changes the App Store submission

This is worth reading before anything gets built. APP_STORE_COMPLIANCE.md section 2.5 names this exact feature as the thing that would break the current answer: “if anyone later proposes showing prayer requests to a group, or a leader dashboard that reads members' journal entries, that single change pulls the entire weight of Guideline 1.2 into scope.” Notes visible to the whole room are user generated content shown to other users, so 1.2 applies and the submission gets meaningfully harder.

What 1.2 then requires, none of which exists today:

  • Terms of service with a no objectionable content clause, agreed to before a first post. The compliance doc currently has ToS as optional and first to cut.
  • A report control on every note, and a way to block a user.
  • A filtering mechanism, and published contact information for abuse reports.
  • A commitment to remove reported content and eject the poster inside 24 hours.

Two smaller consequences ride along. The privacy policy gains a class of data that is shared with other people and needs a retention and deletion answer, and the compliance matrix row that reads User generated content: No flips to yes, which the review notes currently lean on. The host takedown control drawn on the right hand phone is the start of the answer, not the whole of it.

Where the tab bar starts to hurt

The design system asks for four to five tabs and this makes six. The phones above are 375 across, a real iPhone in points, so these are measurements rather than estimates: a sixth tab takes each tile from 67.8pt down to 56.2pt. Tap targets stay legal. The label is what runs out of room, and Connect is the one that does it, setting 48.5pt wide at 11px and leaving under 4pt of air on each side. The mockup renders that honestly rather than quietly shrinking the type.

If that reads badly on a real phone, the fallback is not a smaller font. It is putting the room inside the Guide tab, where the questions already live, and surfacing it as a banner across the top of the guide whenever a room is open. Same feature, no sixth tab, and the guide is where people are already looking on a Thursday night.

Keeping the offline promise

js/content.js opens with a rule the whole app is built on: never blank, never blocked on the network. A shared room is the first screen that genuinely needs a connection, and living rooms with one bar are the normal case rather than the edge case.

The honest version is to cache the room exactly like guides are cached. With no signal the tab still opens, shows the questions and every note it had already seen, marks itself as behind, and holds your own note until the phone can post it. What it must never do is show an empty screen and call it a room.

Nothing here is wired into the app. The phones run the tokens from css/tokens.css and the discussion questions from js/data.js, so what you are looking at is the real type at the real size, on the guide for The Table of Grace.
