The side rail

The scrollbar comes off every page, and the right edge becomes an index of that page's own headings. Press and drag inside the last 34px on the right of the phone — thumb or trackpad, both work.

try What you are looking at

Put a thumb down in the strip and the page's whole contents fades up: every heading, written out in a column to the left of your hand. The one under the finger comes forward onto a card and the page glides to it; its neighbours swell part way; the rest stay small and faint, so you can see where else there is to go without letting go. Tap without dragging and it jumps. Let go and it all fades a second later.

Nothing here is wired into js/ or css/. It is the drawing the change gets decided from.

1 The scrollbar comes off, the scrolling does not

scrollbar-width: none and a zero-width ::-webkit-scrollbar on .hc-scroll, which is the app's one scroll container. Wheel, trackpad, thumb and momentum are all untouched. On a phone this changes nothing visible; on the desktop build it is the difference between the design and a grey bar down the side of it.

2 It is not there until a thumb asks for it

A transparent 34px strip runs down the right edge, from under the header to just clear of the gold back-to-top disc. A pointer landing in it raises the rail in 120ms. Lift the thumb and it holds for one second, then fades over 380ms — long enough to see where you landed, short enough that it is gone before you have read a line.

Reaching for a scrollbar that is not drawn is the gesture this is listening for, so the strip is placed where that reach lands rather than where a scrollbar would have been.

3 The fisheye

Each notch is a hairline pinned to the right edge that grows leftward. Under the finger it runs to its full 30px at full ink; away from the finger it falls off on a gaussian, exp(-(d/46)^2), back to its resting 9px or 6px. Both lengths reach the same 30px at the centre of the swell, so the ruler stays a ruler and the one you are on is still unambiguous. That is one smooth curve rather than a step, so the swell moves through the notches instead of hopping between them.

The same f drives the written headings beside them — scale from 0.78 to full, ink from 0.26 to solid — so the notch and its name are one movement and not two that nearly agree.

Width is drawn with scaleX off a fixed 30px, never by setting width. Nothing in the gesture touches layout.

4 The whole contents page, clear of the hand

Every heading is written out at its own notch's height, right-aligned in a column 46px clear of the edge — well inside the screen, well outside the thumb. The focused one sits at full size and full ink on a card of the tab bar's glass, sized to that heading and moved with it. The rest hold at 0.26 ink and 0.78 scale: readable enough to choose from, plainly behind.

Under all of it the paper comes back over the right of the screen on a gradient with no edge. Without that the page's own headings and the rail's are the same words in the same face on top of each other, and neither reads. The type in the column is the section header's: Poppins 800, one size down, so the rail says what you will land on in the face you will land in.

The column squeezes with the notches. Under about 26px of pitch the faint headings scale down with the gap so a long guide's contents stay a column rather than a pile.

5 Both directions

Rail to page: the notch nearest the finger is the target, and the page eases toward it every frame rather than jumping, so a fast drag reads as flying down the page instead of cutting between stills.

Page to rail: an ordinary scroll keeps the active notch in step — the last heading to have passed the reading line, 72px below the header.

A tap with no drag jumps to the notch under it. A drag that turns out to be mostly sideways is handed back: past 10px of travel with horizontal beating vertical by 1.2×, the rail lets go so js/swipe.js can still take you to the next tab from the right edge.

6 Why it stays smooth

Every stop is measured once, when the thumb goes down. After that a single requestAnimationFrame loop writes two compositor properties per notch — transform and opacity — and skips any write that would move a value by less than 0.004. No reads, no classes, no layout, one loop that stops itself when the finger is still and the page has arrived.

The finger position is itself eased toward the raw pointer (0.35 per frame), which is what takes the tremor out of the swell without adding lag you can feel.

7 Minimal on purpose

No track, no groove, no thumb, no chrome, no panel. The rail is the app's own section-header type at low opacity, one card of the tab bar's glass, and paper drawn back over the page. It borrows nothing from the gold disc, which stays the only gold thing on the screen.

open What the right edge costs

34px of the right edge stops being content while this is on, and vertical travel inside it belongs to the rail rather than to the page. Cards have 20px of padding there so nothing tappable is under it today, but the full-bleed carousel on Home is, and so is the swipe between tabs — which is why a gesture that turns out to be sideways is handed straight back.

The notches sit over whatever the page is showing. On paper and on a card that is nothing; over a photograph, 0.20 ink may want a halo the way the disc's arrow has one.
