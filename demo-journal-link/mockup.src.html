The Seat at the Table

What a forgotten grandson of a dead king teaches us about grace

Allen Barrera · November 3 · 2 Samuel 9

David finally has everything. The wars are quiet, the throne is steady, the kingdom is his. And in the middle of all that, he asks a question nobody asked him to ask. Is there anyone left of Saul’s house I can show kindness to?

They find a man named Mephibosheth. He is Jonathan’s son, crippled in both feet since he was five years old, living in a town called Lo-debar, which means no pasture. It is the middle of nowhere on purpose. He has spent his whole adult life staying small and staying hidden from the man who is now sending soldiers to bring him in.

What he gets is a table. David restores every acre of Saul’s land, assigns a household to work it, and seats Mephibosheth with the king’s own sons for the rest of his life. Here is the line most of us miss. The chapter ends by telling us he was still lame in both feet. Grace did not fix his legs. It changed his address, and it changed what he was called.

These are yours. They save to your account and turn up in your Journal, and nobody else can see them.

1

Lo-debar means no pasture. What is your Lo-debar, the place you go small and stay quiet so nobody looks too closely?

2

Mephibosheth names himself before David can. What is the name you use for yourself when you are being honest?

3

Where are you still trying to be useful enough to keep a seat that was never based on your usefulness?

4.5:1 is the floor for type this size. Brand gold is a colour for a gold shape, not for ink on paper, which is the one thing this page is here to settle. C and D are measured against their own glass composited over the guide, not against the paper.

Where it sits, exactly

Bottom is --hc-tabbar-space, the same line the discs sit on, so the three share a baseline rather than nearly sharing one. The slot runs the full width between them, less a disc and one --hc-space-sm at each end, so a thumb aiming at the link can never clip an arrow.

It is centred in that slot, not in the phone. Those are the same number here and stop being the same number the moment anything else joins the row.

The gold problem

The discs are not gold type, they are gold objects. --hc-gold is the skin; the arrow drawn on it is --hc-gold-ink, which is near black in light and paper in dark. Set type in --hc-gold on the guide’s own paper and it measures 1.70:1, which fails at any size. In the dark it is fine, which is the trap: it looks right on the phone of whoever checks it in bed.

So “the same gold as the arrows” splits in two. C and D keep the literal gold by making it a surface again. A, B, E, F, G keep it as ink and have to deepen it until it reads. Switch The gold to Brand and watch the number go red.

It floats, and it stays

Painted on the phone, not in the scroller, so the guide passes behind it exactly as it passes behind the plinth and the discs. It never travels with the page and it never rides away under a thumb. In the app that is the same fixed layer .hc-disc already lives on, one z-index below the toast.

The back disc is up the whole time you are in a guide; only the top arrow is a scroll state, at TOTOP_AT = 240 in js/app.js. Always up in the switches is the sticky reading and the default here. With the top arrow is the alternative, and it costs a first screenful where the row is one lone arrow on the left. Both go down in presentation mode with everything else, on the same chromeless line.

Where it goes

The Journal lives behind ••• and is a stop, not a pushed view. The router already carries an id, so the tap is HC.router.go({ name: 'journal', id: guide.id }).

What it needs on the other end is a guide filter: js/screens/journal.js groups by guide already, so landing on that group is a fourth pill in FILTERS and a scroll to the heading, rather than a new screen. Nobody should arrive at the top of a stranger’s whole journal.

What is faked

Three sections rather than six, two of them open so there is prose under the link while it scrolls. The index rail on the right edge is drawn rather than live. The tab bar does not navigate, and the link itself does not go anywhere; it is here to be looked at and tapped, not followed.

Everything else is shipping: the tokens unedited, the 20px gutter, the 12px insets, the 44px discs, the 52px bar, and the gold in both themes.

What it cannot settle

Whether a third gold thing costs the discs their findability. css/tokens.css says being the only gold thing on the screen is what makes the disc work without a label, and every study here spends some of that.

And whether the words should say my. The app writes to you in the second person everywhere else, and the Journal calls itself yours. Try both in the switches and read them in a hand.
